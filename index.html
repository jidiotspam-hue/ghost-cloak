1. Overview of Mitochondrial ATP Synthesis

Cellular respiration is a collection of metabolic reactions that convert biochemical energy from nutrients into adenosine triphosphate (ATP), subsequently releasing cellular waste products.

The core catabolic pathways involved include glycolysis in the cytoplasm, pyruvate decarboxylation, the tricarboxylic acid (Krebs) cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation driven by the electron transport chain on the inner mitochondrial cristae membrane.

2. Key Regulatory Checkpoints

Phosphofructokinase-1 (PFK-1) serves as the primary committed rate-limiting step of glycolysis. It is allosterically inhibited by high ATP and citrate levels, and positively regulated by AMP and fructose-2,6-bisphosphate under low cellular energy charge.

Complex IV (cytochrome c oxidase) catalyzes the final transfer of electrons to molecular oxygen, generating water and maintaining the proton motive force across the inner membrane.

3. Chemiosmotic Coupling & ATP Synthase

The proton gradient generated across the inner mitochondrial membrane creates an electrochemical potential driving protons back through the F₀F₁-ATP synthase complex, powering rotational catalysis to phosphorylate ADP into ATP.
